AIM:
To capture Real-time Transport Protocol (RTP) traffic from a VoIP or streaming session and analyse packet delay (jitter), packet loss, and out-of-order delivery using Wireshark.

ALGORITHM:
1. Start Wireshark and capture traffic on the interface used by the call.
2. Place a VoIP call between two endpoints or open an existing VoIP capture file.
3. Allow media traffic to flow for at least 20-30 seconds, then stop the capture.
4. If RTP is not detected automatically, use Decode As on a UDP media packet and select RTP.
5. Open Telephony > RTP > RTP Streams.
6. Select the required stream and click Analyze.
7. Examine sequence number, Delta, Jitter, Skew, and Status fields.
8. Record total packets, lost packets, maximum jitter, and mean jitter.
9. Optionally use Play Streams and inspect RTCP packets.

SOFTWARE REQUIRED:
- Computer with active Ethernet or Wi-Fi interface.
- Wireshark 3.x or later with Npcap installed.
- A VoIP/streaming session or an existing RTP capture file.
- Administrator privileges for packet capture.

THEORY / METRICS
- Packet loss: missing sequence numbers in the RTP stream.
- Jitter: variation in packet inter-arrival time.
- Out-of-order / late packets: packets received after their expected playback position.
- Delta: elapsed time since the previous packet in the stream.
- Skew: drift between expected and actual packet arrival.

Useful Wireshark display filters:

rtp
rtcp
rtp.ssrc == 0x12345678
rtp.marker == 1
sip

The manual provides an example observation: G.711 (PCMU), 1500 and 1498 packets, 6 (0.40%) and 2 (0.13%) lost packets, and mean jitter of 6.2 ms and 4.9 ms. These are sample values only; record measurements from your own capture.

RESULT:
An RTP stream was captured and analysed in Wireshark. Packet delay (jitter), packet loss, and out-of-order delivery were measured using RTP Streams.
